GARAGE / UPDATE ON SI / OCTOBER 7, 2026

Back on the Road.
And a Two-Year Mystery.

A nerve-racking drive to the mechanic, an unexpected alternator diagnosis, a bad $300 battery, and one loose wire that may have finally solved my subwoofer problem.

The drive to Eddie

After replacing the battery, the negative battery ground cables, and a corroded positive battery terminal, the Si still wouldn't start reliably. I finally decided it was time to bring it to Eddie, my mechanic. I was nervous the whole drive, having needed a jump start just to get going. Every time I approached a stop sign or traffic light, I edged toward the right side of the road in case the car stalled and other drivers needed to pass.

When I finally made it to Eddie's shop, I was relieved. He greeted me, listened to everything I'd already tried, and initially suspected the starter. He told me Hondas are great cars, but starter replacements are common in his experience.

Not the starter after all

Eddie asked me to connect the jumper pack and tried starting the car himself. What he observed changed his diagnosis: the problem appeared to be the alternator, with the car not receiving proper charging voltage. He reassured me he'd take care of it, and I walked home feeling like a weight had been lifted.

The next day, Eddie called with the update: a new alternator had been installed. He also discovered that the expensive $300 battery I'd recently purchased was no good. I asked him to install a replacement battery so I could return the defective one for a refund. I paid the repair invoice, picked up the Si, and drove away. The car felt amazing — finally back to normal.

A 50-mile test drive

To make sure everything was working well, I took the Si out toward The Cheesecake Factory on Long Island for a roughly 50-mile test drive. The longer drive also gave the car some running time after the battery reconnection, while I considered the ECU readiness monitors and emissions system. The drive was fantastic.

There was still one disappointment: the subwoofer continued cutting in and out, a problem I'd been dealing with for about two years. I'd hoped the new alternator might fix it, but it didn't seem to.

One loose wire. Two years.

Before taking my daughter to Kings Plaza to pick up a birthday gift for my wife, I opened the trunk to inspect the audio setup. And there it was: a wire disconnected from the back of the subwoofer box. Could this have been the problem all along?

I reconnected the wire and headed to Kings Plaza. This time the subwoofer kept playing without cutting out after a few minutes, as it had done before. It's looking promising — and if it stays that way, a simple connection may have solved a two-year mystery.

Where things stand

The alternator has been replaced, the defective battery swapped out, and the Si is starting and driving normally again. The subwoofer is working so far after reconnecting the loose wire. After all the troubleshooting, the stress of getting to the shop, and the unexpected discoveries, it feels great to enjoy driving the Si again.
