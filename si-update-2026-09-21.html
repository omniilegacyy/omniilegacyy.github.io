GARAGE JOURNAL / 2008 CIVIC SI

September 21, 2026 • Evening

Update on SI.

What started as a simple no-start turned into a full troubleshooting session — and a reminder that sometimes the first part you replace isn't the part causing the problem.

IssueEngine would not start
First StepBattery replaced
Battery CostAbout $300
BreakthroughGround bypass test

The no-start.

I was getting ready to take my mom and her dog to the vet when the Si suddenly wouldn't start. My first thought was the battery, so I replaced it. About $300 later, I had a fresh battery — but the car still wouldn't start.

At least the new battery wasn't a total loss. With winter coming, having a fresh battery is one less thing to worry about.

Process of elimination.

After the battery didn't solve it, I went back to troubleshooting. I watched several videos, compared different no-start symptoms, and worked through possible causes instead of continuing to replace parts.

One troubleshooting video focused on corroded or high-resistance ground connections. That gave me something simple to test before going any further.

The ground-wire test.

We connected one end of a jumper cable to the battery's negative terminal and the other end to clean metal on the vehicle, creating a temporary alternate ground path.

Then came the surprise: the Si started.

That test strongly points to excessive resistance or a poor connection somewhere in the vehicle's normal ground path — potentially a corroded cable, ground strap, or connection point. The jumper cable was only a diagnostic bypass, not a permanent repair.

What's next.

The next step is to inspect the battery-to-chassis and engine/transmission ground connections, clean the mounting points, check the cables and straps for corrosion or damage, and repair or replace the affected ground connection as needed.

For now, the important part is that the troubleshooting test gave me a clear direction — and the Si fired back up.
